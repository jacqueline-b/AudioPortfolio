Multi-Camera Television Production

NYU FMTV-UT 51 Sight & Sound: Studio. Live multi-cam. Production audio, ADR, actors.

Overview

Tisch Sight & Sound: Studio. Live multi-cam narrative, three projects of increasing complexity, seats rotating (director, camera, TD, AD, lights, stage manager). I spent the most time on lavs, live mix, ADR, and working with actors on the floor and in the booth.

Contributions

  • Wired and concealed lavs, set levels and FX before roll. In the booth: watched levels and mixed live from the script (dialogue, yells, slams, on-set FX, music). Recorded split tracks for editorial.
  • Casting posts, reels and self-tapes, rehearsal, and directing actors on a live floor with the same set manners as a working crew.
  • ADR a day or two after the shoot: highlighted scripts for both of us, room ready, Pro Tools for fast takes, picture on monitors, comms from control, files named, talent through the booth.
  • Class rotated director, camera, TD, AD, lighting, and stage manager (comms, countdown, shot lists, studio lights, set). This page is production audio, ADR, and actors.

Challenges

Live mix has no undo. Levels, lavs, and talent all move at once, and you still pick up ADR later when the studio take isn’t usable.

Lessons Learned

Mic someone, mix a live room, run ADR with picture. Sitting camera or director made it obvious when the audio wasn’t working for everyone else.
