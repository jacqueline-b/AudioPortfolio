Multi-Camera Television Production

NYU FMTV-UT 51 Sight & Sound: Studio — live multi-camera narrative. Production audio, ADR, and working with actors.

Overview

NYU Tisch Sight & Sound: Studio — live multi-cam narrative, three projects of increasing complexity, with seats rotating (director, camera, TD, AD, lights, stage manager). Production audio and performance: lavs, live mix, ADR, and working with actors on the floor and in the booth.

Selected contributions

  • Production audio: wired and concealed lavs, set levels and FX before roll, mixed live from the script (dialogue, yells, slams, on-set FX, music), and recorded split tracks for editorial.
  • On-set talent: posted casting, reviewed reels and self-tapes, rehearsed, and directed actors on a live floor with the same professional set manners as a working crew.
  • ADR: a day or two after the studio shoot — scripts and highlighted lines, room ready, Pro Tools for fast takes, comms from control to the live room, picture on monitors, files named and organized, talent in and out professionally.
  • Also rotated director, camera, TD, AD, lighting, and stage manager — comms, countdown, shot lists, studio lights, set — so I know how audio sits in a live show, not only in the booth.

Challenges

Live mix has no undo: levels, lavs, and talent all move at once, and you still have to catch ADR later when the studio take isn’t usable.

Lessons Learned

Games live on performance and ADR the same way this class did. Knowing how to mic someone, mix a live room, and run a session with actors is the transferable part — the other crew seats taught me why that audio has to work for camera and director too.
