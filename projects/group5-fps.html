Group5 FPS

8-week Unity FPS. Lead programmer: sprint assignment, code review, QA, and Git merges to a shipped MVP.

GitHub jakurll is an old GitHub account that belongs to me.

Overview

Six-person student team plus two leads (designers and programmers). Design and technical docs first, then weekly sprints. Professor every two weeks. Playable MVP at eight weeks.

Contributions

  • Assigned weekly programming tasks to three programmers by strength, and took a gameplay or systems task myself.
  • Met about three days a week for about an hour to look over progress and unblock people. Discord for updates between meetings. Lead designer came to me for tools, mechanics, and programming problems.
  • Night before standup: tested each programmer’s work in private, wrote repro notes. Next day, 1:1s on those notes. Then checked in with the lead designer, then the whole group on the coming week. Merge to main on GitHub after that, and fix whatever broke.
  • Professor reviews every two weeks. Deliverables on mechanics, levels, or how far we were. Hit MVP at eight weeks and shipped it.

Challenges

Programmer issues and designer tickets at the same time, while I was still writing major parts of the game. I was the main QA before merge. Catching bugs there is slow. Meeting the deadline sometimes meant using my own time to fix other people’s work.

Once a designer merged to main off-schedule. Engine version didn’t match everyone else’s. Main broke. People had already pulled before we caught it, because it happened on an off day. Next meeting we went through it: no merge without my say. I keep a backup branch for this. Restore was straightforward; some people still lost progress.

Lessons Learned

How to assign work people can actually do and want to do, and use what they’re good at. How to sit with someone’s code, go through bugs and delays, and still treat them like they belong on the team.
