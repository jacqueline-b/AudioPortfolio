New York University, BFA Film & Television — August 2026

At NYU I learned how story and craft have to sit together. Film, TV, games: the art is why you make the thing, and the technical work is how it actually exists. One without the other is a raft with no paddles. You have to know what you want to make, or who you’re making it for. That decision sits under everything after.

Audio is easy to miss until it isn’t there. It finishes a picture — more present, more of a place — or it tells you something the image never shows: a room next door, something coming, something already over. It works on you as a body, not only as a viewer. That’s the part I want to do well.

The other half is the back end. Ideas don’t show up on screen by themselves. Programming, cameras, a crew, the limits of an engine or a shoot: that’s the scaffolding. Doing that work is how I find out what an idea is even allowed to be, and how to build it so someone can sit with it in real time.
