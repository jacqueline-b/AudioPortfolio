New York University, BFA Film & Television — August 2026

At NYU, I learned that artistic and technical craft are inseparable. It begins with what you want someone to experience, and continues with figuring out how to make that experience possible. And this all depends on what you’re making, who it’s for, and what you want it to do to them.

Audio is easy to miss until it isn’t there. It can make a place feel inhabited, give weight to an action, or tell you something the image never shows: A fight in a room next door, a creature approaching from behind, or the comforting crackle of a campfire after a long journey. In games, sound can do more than accompany an experience. It can respond to the player and make a world feel much more rich beyond what’s on screen. That’s the part I want to do well.

I’m interested in both sides of that process: designing sound and building the systems that bring it to life. Programming, audio middleware, game engines, and implementation aren’t separate from the creative work; they’re how an idea becomes something a player can encounter in real time. I want to make audio that is expressive in its own right, technically considered, and inseparable from the worlds it helps create.
